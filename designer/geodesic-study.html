Ten figures. One angle.

Geodesic is a clock figure set drawn for this face. Each figure is a single stroke of constant weight: bowls are true circles, and every diagonal runs at exactly 60°, the angle of the icosahedral net's own triangle edges. The 4, 6, 7 and 9 share that angle with the map beneath them.

The watch strip

200 × 64 pixels · 56-pixel figures in 34-pixel tabular cells · the same lattice as the minute flip

Fixed cells keep the readout still from minute to minute, so the 400 ms triangle flip only moves the figures that change.

Construction

Centre-line skeleton over the approved 1-bit masters, with 60° guides
  • One weight. 14 units of a 100-unit cap height: a 7.8-pixel stem that stays open at 56 pixels.
  • Circles for bowls. The 2, 3, 5, 6, 8 and 9 are built on circles, a quiet echo of the globe the map unfolds.
  • 60° for everything else. The 1's flag is cut vertically into a small lattice triangle; the 4 and 7 run at the net's own angle, and the 6 and 9 leave their bowls on a 60° tangent.
  • Sampled once. The generator samples each skeleton at 8×8 per pixel and freezes the result. The workshop and the watch then draw the same approved masters.
